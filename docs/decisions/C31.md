# C31

Section: C. The counted-claim model

## Decision

An obligation learns what its companions are called as well as where they are, per learned root, with the declared suffix as the prior

## Why

C9 learns the root and left the spelling hardcoded. empire-flippers/api writes the plurality of `spec/models` as `<name>_model_spec.rb`, 52 of 166 against 46 bare, so the row read 46 of 166 over the roster and 44 of 129 over the area, and the map told an agent this repository does not spec its models at half the real rate.
It reads 95 and 93 now.
A candidate spelling is whatever a corpus file adds after a producer's whole tail, never one holding a separator, since `spec/models/address/foo_spec.rb` is a file in a directory named after the producer rather than a file named after it.
Kept at a fifth of the root's producers and at least three, which is a measured bar rather than a chosen one.
Run over the 35-repository corpus, five spellings clear the floor of three: openfoodnetwork's `_rake_spec` at 5 of 15 and empire-flippers/api's `_model_spec` at 52 of 166, both real, and three readings of openproject's `_integration_spec`, the largest 28 of 479.
So a fifth admits the two and the nearest thing it refuses sits under a sixteenth, and that one is a different file's spec caught by its own name, `user.rb` beside `user_membership_spec.rb`.
A spelling has to begin at a separator, `_`, `.` or `-`: `m0.rb` beside `m0book_spec.rb` is `m0book`'s spec, and on a small root the floor and the share coincide so the noise bar alone cannot tell a spelling from a longer name.
The corpus is walked once and each companion asked which producer it extends, rather than each producer asked which companions extend it; the second shape is a scan of the whole corpus per producer and measured 4.7 seconds a row on a 20,000-file repository against 0.9 before the learning existed.
A producer conforms when any learned spelling is in the tree; the declared one is still what a missing companion is reported as, because that is the spelling the row is written with
