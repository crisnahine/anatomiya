# A41

Section: A. Delivery

## Decision

A whole-file decline still owes the reader the count where the file is a site the check enforces over, and that count goes on the counts line rather than on one of its own

## Why

A35 dropped both naming rows for length and left its whole-file reasoning standing over them: a decline that drops a whole file is one `N of N sites across X of Y files` already discloses.
That holds while the dropped file is simply not a site.
It does not hold for `file_naming_case`, and the row is the one place the two sides of the tool disagree by construction: the scan votes with a stem's class and the check enforces over the site, so a stem spelling none of the four is a site with no vote.
Measured on a Rails map of 159 areas, `db/migrate` printed "1525 of 1525 sites across 1525 of 1532 files" while three double-underscore migrations were sites the scan never classified, and a new one there is reported MUST-FIX against that sentence with "all 1525 baseline sites conform" printed beside it.
It was the only disagreement to survive a re-count of all 261 stated claims in that map, and no count in it was wrong: the failure is disclosure, not arithmetic, which is why the fix carries a number rather than correcting one.
Three shapes were tried before this one.
A fixed clause on its own line, the way the other twenty rows carry one, prints in every area stating the row including the 135 of 136 that skipped nothing, and says only that something might be missing, which is a disclaimer where the reader is owed a disclosure.
A clause carrying its own count fixes that and still costs a line: measured across 35 repositories, 2.8% of directories hold such a name and 12.6% of Angular's do, and on vscode six of 500 areas sit at the forty-line bound with three of those stating the row, so the line pushed a stated convention out of one of them and `check` then capped that slot at FIX.
A disclosure that costs a convention is a bad trade.
The count on the counts line costs nothing, sits beside the population it qualifies, and needs no template, no clause and no second printing rule: a declined site never reaches the `except` list, so no perfect-claim guard applies to it either.
The count is narrowed by `splitBy` alongside the population it belongs to, or it would name files the sentence above it no longer speaks about, and it is stored because the record is what the map is derivable from, the way `companionsElsewhere` is.
A count the renderer cannot read as a whole number above zero prints nothing, since a record is a file on disk
