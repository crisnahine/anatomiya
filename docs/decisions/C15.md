# C15

Section: C. The counted-claim model

## Decision

Every registry row carries `kind`, one of three, and the whole load battery runs over all three lists, pairings included

## Why

A row was one of six shapes told apart by which optional fields exist, so every reader re-derived the union: H14's one boolean cost four modules, H12 shipped learned rows the map stated and the check silently did not enforce, and `PAIRINGS` passed through three of the five asserts, so a pairing spelling its tier `"Syntactic"` would have loaded and been dropped by every reader without a word.
The kind is how a row is counted, a tree walk, a corpus fold or a companion match; a tree row is stamped at load rather than spelled per row, and the newest fields (`groupedSites`, `noneClaim`, `learnedFromSource`, `capability`, `framework`, `blindWhenStripped`) are held to shape the way the first five always were.
The check finds its corpus rows by kind, never by a key literal, so a second corpus row is enforced the day it is declared.
Two things deliberately not unified: the reducer's grouped fold and the check's fold share `groupKey` and the one-vote-per-body rule and sit on their own sides of the counts/findings split, and the facts record keeps inferring a slot's kind from which counts are present until a reader needs more, which is a schema bump away
