# B56

Section: B. Engine

## Decision

A claim line names the extensions it was counted over wherever its area holds three or more source files of languages the row is never asked of. Whether the clause prints is decided by language and by that count, and what it names is the files the row was asked of

## Why

An area delivers on one glob for every language in it, so a claim learned from a directory's `.ts` files reaches an agent editing the `.svelte` file beside them, where the row was never asked and the opposite may be the rule.
Measured before the clause: across kit's 79 component areas, 67 stated or default-matching slots belonged to rows not asked of Svelte, such as `module-level bindings are const: 7 of 7 sites` over a glob that also covers eight `.svelte` files, which open with `let { data } = $props()`; element-plus held 24 such slots and shadcn-svelte 4.
The clause sits on the sentence and not on a line under it, because the sentence is the one part every form of a slot prints: the directive, the counts line, and the bare sentence the budget notice keeps (A34).
So it costs no line.
Each line that carries the clause is the bare line plus the clause, and no file gains or loses a line: counted on clones of the 35 corpus repositories, each map rendered with the clause and without, 0 of the changed lines is anything else and 0 files change length.
The clause is not limited to one family: a claim over a glob that covers files it was never asked of is ambiguous whatever the two languages are.
On the component repositories kit carries 66 clauses in 53 of 110 area files and element-plus 67 in 23 of 113, and `check` agrees with the clause: under `module-level bindings are const, in .js and .ts files`, a new `.svelte` file's two top-level `let` are not reported and a new `.js` file's are.
The names come from the files the fold asked, after the JSX and type-syntax filters.
Naming them from the language ids was measured and refused: element-plus printed `, in .ts and .tsx files` on 16 lines of JSX-only rows that no `.ts` file was asked, where it prints `, in .tsx files`.
The floor of three is `PRECEDENT_FLOOR`, the number of files a directory needs before its tests are read as a precedent, counted per row over the files of every language that row is never asked of.
With no floor, one stray file put the clause on every claim line of a large area: Homebrew's `Library/Homebrew` holds 220 Ruby files and one `.py`, and cal.diy's `packages/app-store` 250 JavaScript and TypeScript files and one `.php`.
Counted on clones of the 35 corpus repositories with no floor: 892 lines in 104 of 6,947 area files, 16 repositories, carry the clause, and 41 of the 104 hold a line whose row was not asked of one or two files, which is over the third that was set as the bar for a floor.
With the floor, 566 lines in 85 area files of 14 repositories carry it: 19 files lose every clause, 22 keep it only on the rows of the language that holds the one or two files, since those rows were not asked of the many, and 326 lines print bare.
461 of the 566 lines sit in 50 areas holding Ruby beside JavaScript, where no row is asked of both, 59 in 26 areas of storybook and supabase that hold components beside modules, and 46 in 9 areas that hold a file of the seven languages beside JavaScript or Ruby.
Counted on copies of the same trees, as the comparison with 0.13.4 was, it is 567 lines: mastodon prints 18 where a clone prints 17, since the author gate reads a clone's history and a copy's differently and one more slot prints its line
