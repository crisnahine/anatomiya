# B76

Section: B. Engine

## Decision

No kill in the library is sent to a child that has no pid

## Why

A child whose `spawn` or `fork` failed has a handle and no pid until Node's next-tick queue runs, and `kill()` on it in that window sends the signal to pid 0, which is the whole process group of the caller: the scan and the shell that started it, or a hook's group.
Measured on Node 22.22.3 on macOS, each probe detached in a process group of its own, from `spawn` and from `fork`: with no file descriptor left (EMFILE) and with a binary or a working directory that is not there (ENOENT), the process that sent `SIGKILL` died of it.
So descriptor pressure is not needed: a missing interpreter or a removed temp directory is enough.
A promise continuation queued in the tick of the start is still inside the window.
One next tick later, and inside the child's own `'error'` listener, the handle is gone, and `kill()` returns false and sends nothing.
Two callers could reach the window: `runSemantic` settles at once where its caller's signal is already aborted, and a pool closed in the tick its workers were forked.
A worker that never started never emits `'exit'` either, so a close that waited on one would wait for good.
The guard was written by hand at eight `.kill(` sites in four files, each with its own wording of the reason, and nothing failed when one more was written without it

## Status

**done** `child.mjs` (`signalChild`, `guardedChild`), `pool.mjs` (`createPool`), `ruby.mjs` (`onThread`, `startFor`, `killAndReap`), `git.mjs` (`blobReader`, `gitStreamed`), pinned by `test/child.test.mjs` (`a kill in the tick of a start that failed signals nothing`, `a kill of a child a spawner stands in for is the stand-in's to send`) and `test/pool.test.mjs` (`a pool closed in the tick its workers failed to start signals nothing and answers`), each helper detached with a descriptor limit of its own, and by `test/modules.test.mjs` (`a signal goes to a child from the one function that sends it only where the child started`).
`signalChild` is the one function that sends a signal to a child: it answers false and sends nothing for no child and for a `ChildProcess` with no pid, and sends for anything else, since what a `spawner` hands back stands in for a process held on another thread, has no pid, and sends its own kill.
Every signal goes through it: both kills of `guardedChild`, the caller's and a clock's, the clock and the stop of a streamed git read, `blobReader`'s kill, both signals of a pool's `close`, and the Ruby bridge's three.
The test reads every line of the library and fails on a `.kill(` outside `signalChild`, but for `process.kill(pid, 0)`, which asks whether a pid is alive and sends nothing, and the `kill(reason)` of the handle `guardedChild` returns, which sends through `signalChild`.
The reason a kill was asked for is recorded whether or not a signal went.
A pool's `close()` resolves at once for a worker with no pid, before the kill and before the one-second escalation to `SIGKILL`.
The Ruby thread's kill message is acted on only for a child with a pid, and `killAndReap` resolves for one without.
`blobReader` counts a child as killed only where `signalChild` answers that the signal went (E17)
