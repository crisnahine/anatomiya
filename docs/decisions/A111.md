# A111

Section: A. Delivery

## Decision

At the area ceiling, the places of areas a scan drops for counting nothing go back to the areas the fold took last, the last taken first, in every scan and every pin, and never past twice the plain ceiling, the most A112 lets a pin name. An area holding a language the checker reads (`js`, `jsx`) is never one that counts nothing, a held area keeps its place, and a tie in size folds by each area's first file

## Why

The fold runs before anything is counted, so a place an area gave up by counting nothing was lost: three on next.js and three on canvas-lms.
Measured on copies of the 60 corpus repositories against 0.14.0 (`docs/measurements/2026-10-11-area-ceiling-give-back.md`): four reach the ceiling; next.js goes from 497 areas to 500 and canvas-lms from 497 to 498, which states 967 claims for 962, while vscode and openproject drop no area for counting nothing and do not move, and files in no area move on none of the four.
Two of canvas-lms's three dropped areas hold JavaScript, and those places stay empty: the checker may count rows there, a pin cannot know whether it will run, and calling such an area empty would let a pin and a scan of the same commit part.
A pin on another partition than a scan's makes every split host read as a population change, and the refresh pins most checkouts at the ceiling, so the pin gives back too.
An area that counts but states nothing keeps its place, since ranking by stated claims would move the partition with every commit.
By path a parent folded before its child, and on next.js that moved 4 areas of 16 to 30 files out of the map with nothing given back, so a tie folds by first file, which is 0.14.0's order on input in git's order
