# D8

Section: D. Gates

## Decision

The `evidence` gate passes on the area's own Wilson bound, **or** on the rest of the repository's bound for the same dimension and learned class, when this area's own upper bound reaches the rate it is borrowing. Leave-one-out, pooled per learned class, built only from slots no other condition has closed, and read off the same population every other gate reads

## Why

Of 90 mechanical review comments across 170 merged pull requests on two mapped repositories, the map would have pre-empted none, and a gate was the reason rather than a predicate.
The bound needs about 35 perfect sites to reach 0.90 and the measured front end's median area holds 11 files, so an area could be perfectly consistent and never speak: `interface_prefix` holds 2,125 of 2,152 repo-wide at 0.987, 116 of its 118 areas clear the ratio gate, and six state it.
A reviewer left "we typically prefix our interfaces with an `I`" on a file whose area read `no convention. 9 of 9 sites (evidence)`, bound 0.7008.
A 9-of-9 directory is not weak evidence of a weak claim, it is a consistent sample of a strong one.
Two measured cases the design had to survive and does: `handler_is_named` 3 of 7 in a repository that is 0.757 the other way stays silent on both sides, because `ratio` fires first and the prior lends neither side anything; and `record_lookup` 26 of 26 in a directory of a repository that is 0.539 stays silent, held only by the prior failing at 0.4937, which is the case the design has to earn.
The consistency clause is what earns its place: without it a 900-of-1000 area at exactly the ratio bar, whose own upper bound is 0.917, would inherit a 0.9875 repository's confidence, which is the direction that manufactures conventions.
A repository sitting at exactly 0.90 lends nothing at any size.
The comparison that clause is written as needed a bound that is actually a bound, which D10 supplies.
Measured after the fact on a 2,486-file front end, and the volume is worth knowing before anyone reads a map: stated slots go from 72 to 735, of which 799 of the 947 states are borrowed and none of them borrows against a repository rate under 0.90 on the side it states.
The always-loaded cost falls rather than rises, 240,636 bytes to 225,424, because a stated directive replaces a longer counts line, and every file still holds the 40-line bound.
The thinnest slot that can borrow is 3 sites over 3 files, which is `concentration`'s existing floor rather than a new one; no number was invented for this.
What the reader gains and loses on that repository, measured either side: MUST-FIX-capable slots go from 86 to 82, because a borrowed claim is capped at FIX, and 95 stated slots lose their line to the 40-line budget and are capped by H24 instead, against 19 before.
Printed directives across all 127 areas go from 64 to 714, and exactly one area prints fewer than it did.
So the map says a great deal more and the check says it more quietly, which is the trade this row is

## Status

**done** `gates.mjs` (`wilsonUpper`), `reduce.mjs` (`applyGates`, and `blockOf` split out of `verdictFor` so a closed slot can be excluded before the gates run), `scan.mjs` (two passes over the areas, the pool a sum so two scans of unchanged source still agree), `facts.mjs` (schema 14: `priorBound` and `borrowed` on each side, so a stated claim can be audited from disk for which of the two carried it), `check.mjs` (a borrowed claim is capped at FIX, because MUST-FIX is a statement about this area's own history and that is exactly what was borrowed).
The new coupling is worth saying out loud: an area's map now depends on the rest of the repository, so scanning a subtree answers differently from scanning the whole of it
