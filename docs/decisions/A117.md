# A117

Section: A. Delivery

## Decision

The echo and the refresh worker take their locks through one module, `lock.mjs`: an exclusive create, and a dead lock broken one generation at a time by the call that wins the exclusive create of the lock token that generation names. What makes a lock dead is a judge each caller passes: the echo's reads the lock's mtime against the hook timeout (A92), the refresh worker's reads the pid and the age the lock holds (A95). A lock token whose mtime is more than 62 s from the clock is a dead lock itself, broken the same way one level down and no further

## Why

The echo broke a dead lock through a lock token and tried a refused create again; the refresh worker moved a dead lock aside, had neither, and kept a second copy of the create.
Moving the lock aside let two workers hold it: one worker's rename caught the lock a second had just made, and a third took the name before the first could link it back.
A forced interleave of three gave 2 holders, and started together with lock reads held up to 2 ms and starts spread over 5 ms, 1 of 200 rounds at 3 workers and 11 of 200 at 8 ended with two.
Through the lock token, 200 of 200 rounds at each size ended with one holder and no file beside the lock.
A lock token whose breaker was killed stayed, and every later call found it taken and left that dead lock alone for good.
So a lock token is judged as a lock is, by its mtime: a break takes microseconds, and a minute plus the 2 s a FAT mtime can be off by is far past any breaker still running.
A dead lock token is broken by the same protocol under its own lock token, `<token>.<its mtime in ms>`, and the call that wins goes on as the lock token's holder.
Unlinked by age alone, one old lock token could be removed by two calls, the second taking the fresh lock token the first had just made.
What is left: a breaker suspended for more than 62 s is taken for dead, which A92 accepts of a holder suspended past its bound.
And a breaker killed while it holds a token's own token leaves a lock nothing here breaks, since the protocol stops one level down: it stays until a sweep takes the token's own token, the echo's day-old one or a scan's (A118)

A judge answers one of three things: null where the lock is gone, live, or dead with a generation and an identity.
The generation names the lock token, `<lock>.<generation>`, and the lock is removed only while the judge, asked again by the lock token's holder, still answers dead with the same identity.
Every call then makes the one create of the lock, and a call that loses it does not hold the lock.
`mtimeAge` reads a lock within its bound of the clock, either way, as live; its generation is the mtime in ms and its identity the inode and the mtime in ns, read exactly, since an NTFS file id runs past what a float holds.
`ownerAge` reads a lock as dead where its content does not parse, its pid is no integer or its `at` no number, it is older than its bound or stamped later than now, or its process is gone; its generation is the 16 hex nonce the lock holds, or 16 hex of a digest of what was read where it holds none, and its identity is the kind of entry `readHead` found at the name and, for a file, at most the first 256 bytes of it.
A live answer from `ownerAge` names its generation too, which is how a scan's sweep keeps a live lock's lock tokens (A118).
`createdAlone` given content writes it beside the lock and links it into place, so a reader never sees the lock empty, and takes the plain exclusive create where the link is refused for any reason but a name already there.
An exclusive create Windows refuses with EPERM, its answer for a name another process is still deleting, is tried again seven times after waits doubling from 1 ms to 64 ms, and is thrown after that.
An empty directory at a dead lock's name is removed, and the lock is taken on that call.
A dead lock the system will not remove is thrown, with `blocked` naming it where the entry is no regular file, and a lock token it will not remove is left.
Nothing is removed below the name and no link is followed, so a directory holding entries stays whole.
A lock's entry is read in one place, its kind and at most its first 256 bytes (`lockEntry`, `contentOf`), by the judge and by the release of the refresh lock (`lockContent`).
The worker that takes the refresh lock removes every lock token beside it that names another generation (`removeLeftTokens`), reading at most 256 names.
A directory holding entries at a lock token's name is never removed, whatever its age, and the worker answers `lock-blocked` for it before it takes the lock (A95)
