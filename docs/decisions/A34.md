# A34

Section: A. Delivery

## Decision

A stated convention the line budget cannot print in full is still named, as its sentence alone with no counts, and the notice says how many it could name

## Why

A footer reporting how many facts a reader is missing without saying which is the worst of both: the reader knows a directive exists, has nothing to look up, and conforms to the nine sentences on the page rather than the twelve the check will grade against.
Measured on the same front end: 58 of 127 areas truncated, hiding 438 claim lines, and 36 of those hid 68 stated conventions between them.
`src/queries/users` is the sharpest, 15 files, 9 stated conventions printed and 3 more stated and unprinted.
A sentence costs one line where its block wanted three or more, so naming is strictly denser than printing and the drop walks down from the largest fit rather than settling upward from the smallest.
The naming block takes half the body and no more: unbounded it spent the whole budget on sentences, and a 30-slot area printed no counts at all, which retires the audit D7 exists to provide.
Where the half is not enough the notice leads with how many of them it named, or the list reads as complete and is not.
A named slot is still a dropped slot to `check`, deliberately: what the reader lost is the count, the exceptions and the audit, and the severity that means "the map told you and you are the first to break it" is a statement about evidence the file no longer carries.
So the sentence is delivered and the grading stays capped, and `droppedSlots` holds the key either way.
Counted off those keys and never off the sentences, because `check` recomputes this layout from `facts.json`, which stores every slot's key and no slot's prose: reserving lines off the prose would have the writer keep nine blocks where the check counted ten, and the two would disagree about what the agent was handed
