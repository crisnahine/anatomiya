# D10

Section: D. Gates

## Decision

`wilsonUpper` is held to the two bounds its arithmetic owes and its division does not give: never above one, never below the rate it was handed

## Why

Whether an area states a convention, and therefore whether `check` can ever grade against it, was decided at a perfect ratio by which side of 1 a float happened to land on.
D8's borrow compares `wilsonUpper(k, n)` against the rest of the repository's rate, and that rate is exactly 1 wherever the rest of the repository holds a claim without exception, so the test reduces to `wilsonUpper(n, n) >= 1`.
Unclamped it returned 0.9999999999999998 at 118 of the first 500 sample sizes and 1.0000000000000002 at others, and nothing in the code decided which.
The damage is visible in the shape of the counts: on a 2,489-file front end 172 rows printed a perfect ratio that was still denied, and bucketing them by sample size against the float, 37 of the 172 sat at n = 12, 20, 21 and 31, every one of them a size the ratio fell short at, while n = 16, 17, 19, 23, 24, 25, 26, 29, 30, 32 and 33 produced none between them.
The gate was not measuring evidence at those sizes, it was measuring rounding, and it was not even monotone in n at a fixed ratio, which is the clearest statement of the defect.
Fixed in the function rather than at the call site, because both bounds are true of a Wilson upper bound and neither was being held: the lower clamp is what carries the perfect case, and it subsumes asking separately whether the sample already meets the rate it is borrowing, since at or above that rate the bound is too, by construction rather than by rounding.
This repository holds 38 perfect rows and 3 of them sat at a denied size
