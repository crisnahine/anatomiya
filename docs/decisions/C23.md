# C23

Section: C. The counted-claim model

## Decision

The omission is a site: a filename spelling none of the four naming classes, and a top-level class naming no superclass at all

## Why

The one-sided shape H16 fixed for `module_include`, twice more.
`classifyBasename` answers null for two different things and only one of them is not a site: a name matching every class cannot disagree with any, and a name matching none disagrees with all of them, so `TMP_PROBE_UPPER.rb` and `_tmp_probe.rb` passed a stated snake_case claim while `TmpProbe.rb`, `tmpProbe.rb` and `tmp-probe.rb` were all caught, and the same shape held on the client map.
The strongest reading of "classes here inherit ApplicationRecord" was again the one the check could not enforce, and the realistic violation is again the omission: an agent drops a PORO into `app/models` rather than inheriting the wrong base.
Measured over seven Ruby repositories the superclass omission costs two stated slots and gains two, and both losses are honest: discourse's `plugins/discourse-ai/lib/agents/tools` goes 54 of 54 to 54 of 56 and its `plugins/discourse-workflows/lib/discourse_workflows/nodes` 58 of 58 to 58 of 63, because those directories hold bare classes the old shape could not see.
The largest population it moves states on neither side: forem's `app/models` goes 119 of 132 to 119 of 140 and prints "no convention" before and after.
The report asked for `class TmpOdd < Struct.new(:a)` to count too and the measurement refuses it: a superclass naming no constant is still a superclass somebody chose, and `ActiveRecord::Migration[7.2]` is an index call, so counting it took mastodon's `db/migrate` from 41 of 41 to 41 of 576 and lost the claim in two directories that hold nothing but migrations.
H16's carve-outs map over: a class inside a class is that class's helper, and a reopening declares its superclass in the part that carries it
