# B66

Section: B. Engine

## Decision

A tree-sitter parse the 5 second clock kills is charged on that attempt. It is not parsed again alone, as an oxc parse is under B3

## Why

The slow case measured is the grammar and not the machine.
The Kotlin grammar is quadratic in one shape of `<` comparison, a name on the left and a number, string or character literal on the right, `a < 0`: 1,000 functions of one each (48 KB) parsed in 0.4 s, 2,000 in 1.6 s and 4,000 (195 KB) in 6.4 s, where the 4,000 written with `>` took 0.07 s.
At 2,000 functions `a < b`, `0 < a`, `a <= 0` and `f() < 0` each parsed in under 0.1 s.
A retry alone takes as long as the first parse and adds a whole timeout to the scan for each such file.
Given up: a parse that was slow only because the machine was busy is charged as crashed, and the count of crashed files can then differ between two scans of one tree
