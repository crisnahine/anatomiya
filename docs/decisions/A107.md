# A107

Section: A. Delivery

## Decision

The area ceiling folds the smallest areas first, by file count alone, whatever language they hold and whatever they state. An area of a newly read language takes its place there by size like any other

## Why

The ceiling is `clamp(ceil(N / 16), 120, 500)` over the source files, a bound on what the overview's Areas listing and the map cost to load.
Reading seven more languages raises N and adds areas, and where a repository already sat at 500 the new areas displace smaller ones.
Measured on copies of the 35 corpus repositories against 0.13.4, in which three sit at 500 areas. vscode (12,165 source files there, 12,279 here) holds 500 in both: six areas of 8 files each fold away, which stated 18 claims, and six arrive, four of them over the Rust of `cli/src` (16 to 30 files) and one over 10 `.py` files, none of which states a claim; two kept areas that took folded files in state 4 more, and the scan goes from 1,798 claims stated to 1,784. next.js (20,234 to 21,250) goes from 500 areas to 497: 30 JavaScript and TypeScript areas of 10 or 11 files fold away, which stated 7 claims, and 27 arrive, 24 of them Rust alone and 2 Rust beside JavaScript, none of which states a claim; kept areas gain 2 and lose 1, and 429 claims stated become 423. openproject holds 500 in both and nothing moves.
An area file carries its kinds line, the tests it holds and its counts whether or not it states a claim, so size decides and statement does not
