# C60

Section: C. The counted-claim model

## Decision

`strong_params_style` learns which call a Rails controller's parameter filter starts from, `require`, `permit` or `expect` on `params`, one site per chain

## Why

A reviewer on a Rails API in the corpus asked for `params.permit` over `params.require(...).permit`, and the repository holds it: 91 of the 92 filters in its controllers start with `permit`.
The corpus splits three ways: whitehall starts 41 of 42 with `require`, errbit 16 of 17 with `expect`, and discourse, forem and canvas-lms mix `require` with `permit`.
The generator writes `require(...).permit`, so the repository whose habit is otherwise is the one a model gets wrong.
A `permit` chained off a `require` is that chain's second call, so a chain is one site rather than two votes for both classes.
Partial, because parameters handed to a local or a helper first are filtered through a receiver this does not follow.
Over the corpus it opens 122 slots and states in 10 areas: `expect` in 8 of decidim's and in mastodon's `app/controllers/admin` (37 of 37), and `permit` in a measured Rails API's `app/controllers/api/v1` (57 of 57). A lone `params.require(:id)` reads one value and filters nothing, so it is no site, which is what moved mastodon's area over the bar
