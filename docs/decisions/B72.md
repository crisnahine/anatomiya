# B72

Section: B. Engine

## Decision

The checker's job answers every file's resolution, and where each type-checked row has a site, before it walks a row. A scan that reads a degraded verdict off those answers ends the run there and kills the job, and under a refused config the job walks no row at all. The map is the one a whole walk writes

## Why

A degraded tier keeps no type-checked row (B8), so all a scan reads of the checker's hits is whether a file holds a site: that decides which areas are folded, and so which files the rate is taken over.
Where a row has a site is read off the tree with no type (`siteStubs`), so the verdict is known before any row is walked.
The scan discovers its areas before it waits for the checker, folds them over the stubs, and where the rate over the counted files is under 0.80 the run answers with the stubs and the job is killed, as it is on every ending.
No message goes back to the job: a build that told it to stop and waited for its answer bought nothing over the kill, and a job stuck inside one file then held the scan for up to 60 s.
The scan's own fold decides the verdict again from what the run answered, and a stopped run it reads as anything but degraded throws, since the stubs credit every site as conforming and would print as counted.
Measured on prisma (3,647 checked files, degraded at 0.659), the job's CPU in process, three runs at a load average of 80 to 310: the row walk is 1.9 to 2.1 s of 40.8 to 42.8 s, about 5%, the build 15 to 19 s and the resolution pass 21 to 24 s.
The design notes had inferred about a quarter; the walk does not pay for its type lookups, since whichever pass asks first pays and the verdict needs the resolution pass.
Three builds were measured, and the tree is the third: the one before the two passes, one that told the job to stop by a message and waited for its answer, and the one this row describes, which kills the job.
Scan wall on prisma against the build before the two passes, ten interleaved pairs at a load average of 97 to 422, taken on the build that told the job to stop: the faster of the pair in 10 of 10, medians 80.2 s to 69.2 s over the first five and 56.3 s to 52.9 s over the second.
The build this row describes used the same user CPU as that one to 0.03 s over ten interleaved pairs, and wall could not tell the two apart at a load average of 45 to 390, where single runs spread from 24 s to 104 s.
On the four corpus repositories whose tier is ok (typeorm, webpack, mastodon, react-front-end), three interleaved pairs each at a load average of 20 to 30 on that same earlier build, the scan was equal or faster in 10 of 12 pairs, since area discovery runs while the checker builds.
On the build that told the job to stop, all 60 corpus repositories hashed the same as 0.15.1 and their whole dry-run results were identical: eight run the checker, four degrade, none has a refused config, and none folds an area on a type-checked row alone.
On the build this row describes, the eight that run the checker and four that do not hash the same as 0.15.1, 12 of 12.
Given up: under a degraded verdict a file whose row would have thrown counts as sited, where the walk would have dropped its key; it moves a byte only where an area is folded on a type-checked row alone, which no corpus repository does.
The files the rate is taken over then differ, so near the 0.80 floor a stopped run, or a run under a refused config, can read degraded where a whole walk would not.
Given up: a checker job that dies after it has said where every row has a site, and before the scan's reader answers, is recorded as a failed tier (`tier-failed`) though the degraded verdict was already in hand, so which of the two degraded reasons is written depends on that race, and the map is the same either way

## Status

**done** `dimensions-semantic.mjs` (`siteStubs`, `eachChain`, and `sited` on the row), `semantic-job.mjs` (`runJob`), `semantic.mjs` (`runSemantic`, which takes `stopWhen` and marks the answer `stopped`), `scan.mjs` (`degradedAlready`, `foldedArea`, `countedIn`, and the throw on a stopped run), pinned by `test/semantic-job.test.mjs`, `test/semantic.test.mjs`, `test/scan.test.mjs` and `test/dimensions-semantic.test.mjs`.
A type-checked row that declares no `sited` stops `dimensions-semantic.mjs` loading, by name (`assertSited`).
The job sends `{ built, config }`, then `{ rel, hits: {}, resolution, sites }` per file, then `{ sited }`, then `{ rel, hits, resolution }` per file with what the checker counted, then `{ done }`.
Under a refused config it sends one pass, each file's `hits` the stubs, and no `{ sited }`.
`runSemantic` asks `stopWhen` once, on the first `{ sited }`, with the stubs as hits; on yes it resolves with the stubs and `stopped: true` and kills the child, and on no, on a throw, or where the job finished first, the answer is the walked hits.
A stub is one conforming site with no place, for each row with a site in the file.
`degradedAlready` and the scan's fold ask one function, `foldedArea`, whether an area is folded, and `countedIn` names the files the rate is taken over for both.
`stopped` does not reach the record: the fold classes the tier degraded and its rows leave as B8 has it.
`semantic-worker.mjs` is the shell B39 describes, with no stop message.
A row that throws while it looks for a site leaves no stub in that file, as its walk has no hit there (`siteStubs`)
