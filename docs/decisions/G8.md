# G8

Section: G. Scope

## Decision

The fixture exclusion also matches a word boundary, not only a whole segment: `cases` bare or camelCase-compounded into `...Cases`, `fixture(s)` bare or underscore-compounded, `golden(s)` bare or joined to `test`, `snapshot(s)` bare

## Why

Measured on four repositories. webpack spells its fixture-case directories `cases`, `configCases`, `statsCases`, `watchCases`, `hotCases`, `benchmarkCases`, `memoryLimitCases` and `typesCases`: 347 of 393 areas and 3 of 14 stated directives came from them, and two of those three contradict each other on one dimension, one stating that relative imports carry the file extension at 50 of 50 and the other that they are written without it at 113 of 116. angular's `golden-test` is a hyphenated compound `/goldens?/` never reached; prisma's `_fixture` is singular and underscore-prefixed, 309 source files, and diaspora's `jasmine_fixtures` is the same compound the other way round; next.js spells `tests/fixture` and `tests/snapshot` singular with no underscore at all, 75 of 500 areas

## Status

**done** `corpus.mjs` (`EXCLUDE_DIR`), pinned by `test/corpus.test.mjs`.
The boundary is a literal `-` or `_`, or webpack's own capital-letter join, never a bare substring: `use-cases` and `showcases` are real directory names carrying neither.
Still misses chef's `spec/data` (8 of 70 areas, holding directories named `borken`) and next.js's `tests/execution`, both real words too common to exclude bare, and no compounding is added for `golden` or `snapshot` past `-` and `_`, since only the hyphenated and bare leaks were measured
